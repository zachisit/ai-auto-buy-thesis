## Decision Rationale
Yesterday was the most informative single day of the entire experiment. The market's reaction to the ceasefire revealed everything we need to know about current conditions:

1. **The gap-and-dump in DAL was a textbook distribution event.** DAL opened at $73 on ceasefire euphoria, hit $74.19, then was sold down to $68.08 — a $6 intraday reversal with 100% sell pressure on 20.7M volume. This means institutional investors used the ceasefire rally to exit positions, not initiate new ones. When smart money sells a peace rally, it tells you they don't believe the peace will hold.

2. **Our day-only buy stop at $74.25 was six cents from triggering.** If DAL had pushed to $74.25 instead of $74.19, we would have entered and immediately faced a $6/share collapse. The $74.25 trigger, which was set to confirm the gap would hold, did exactly its job — it didn't confirm because the gap didn't hold. This is the fifth time day-only orders have saved us from a losing trade (NTR 3/19, AM 3/31, AM 4/1, DAL 4/8 — with this being the most dramatic save at ~$80 avoided).

3. **The ceasefire is not yet tradeable.** Iran's parliament claiming violations within 24 hours, the Strait remaining mostly blocked (4 transits), and the divergence between political statements and physical reality means the ceasefire is still rhetoric, not action. Until ships are actually flowing through the Strait in meaningful numbers, the "peace trade" has no foundation.

The correct action is to wait for the Islamabad talks this weekend. If those produce concrete commitments — a mechanism for Strait transit, a timeline for de-escalation, something verifiable — then Monday offers a cleaner entry. If they collapse, we'll know the ceasefire was another head-fake and can position accordingly.

## Lessons / Observations
1. **Day-only buy stop orders are the single best risk management tool in this experiment.** Five saves now: NTR ($38 avoided), AM ×2, and DAL yesterday (~$80 avoided). Total capital preserved by day-only discipline: estimated $150-200+. This is more than any single trade has earned.

2. **Gap-and-dump patterns are the market telling you who's selling and who's buying.** When a stock gaps up 12% on news and closes at the low of the day with 100% sell pressure, institutions are distributing to retail. Never buy into a gap that can't hold.

3. **The ceasefire may be real in political terms but is not yet real in economic terms.** Ships aren't flowing. Oil bounced. The Strait is still controlled by Iran. Until the physical blockade lifts, the economic impact of the war persists regardless of what politicians announce.

4. **The experiment's cumulative performance (-$50.60) would be dramatically worse without day-only order discipline.** If we add up the avoided losses (NTR ~$38, DAL yesterday ~$80+, AM×2 unknown but likely $20-40), the discipline has saved $140-160 in potential additional losses. The real "strategy" of this experiment may be less about picking winners and more about the systematic avoidance of losers through entry confirmation.

5. **PFE's stop-out at $26.55 the day before the ceasefire rally is painful in hindsight.** PFE closed at $27.47 today premarket after rallying on the ceasefire — above our entry. The stop was technically correct (VSA was weakening) but the timing was brutal. This reinforces that stops are a risk management tool, not a prediction tool. Sometimes they save you, sometimes they cost you. Over 20 trades, they should save more than they cost.
