## Lessons / Observations
1. **WHR taught us that VSA health alone doesn't make a winning trade.** Seven consecutive bullish signals with institutional backing, and the stock went nowhere. The system correctly identified support (the range held) but support ≠ markup. Future trades need both system health AND a catalyst within the holding window.

2. **Calendar events should be built into the trade plan from day 1.** The ceasefire expiration was known when we entered WHR on 4/10. The trade should have been structured as "exit by 4/21 regardless" from the start. Instead, we extended based on system signals and got trapped needing catalysts before the deadline.

3. **The ceasefire-neutral approach (ET) may be the experiment's most important strategic evolution.** Every trade except DVN has been implicitly directional on the war outcome. DVN worked because we bet correctly (oil up). DAL, PFE, and WHR lost because they required specific outcomes (peace, defense, recovery) that didn't materialize in time. ET is designed to work regardless of outcome. If this approach succeeds, it validates a fundamentally different way of trading during geopolitical uncertainty.

4. **Position sizing matters more than stock selection in a $1,000 account.** ET at 53 shares gives 3x the dollar exposure of WHR (17 shares) for the same capital. Every $0.10 move in ET = $5.30 vs $1.70 in WHR. This means ET needs a much smaller percentage move to hit our profit targets. The experiment's best trade (DVN at 22 shares × $3.30 = $72.60) would only need a $1.37 move in ET to match.

5. **The day-only order discipline continues to prove its value.** ET's buy stop at $18.95 required the 93% buy bar to have follow-through. It filled — confirming buyers are present. If it hadn't filled, we'd have avoided a position that couldn't confirm momentum. Six saves and counting.
