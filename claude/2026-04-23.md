## Lessons / Observations
1. **The operator's challenge to follow the VSA system was correct.** My instinct was to override the Churning signal because the price action looked bullish. But the experiment's rules exist for a reason — the system has been more reliable than my judgment. Tightening the stop costs nothing if I'm right (price goes up, stop isn't hit), but protects capital if the system is right (price fades, stop catches it). There's no downside to respecting the system.

2. **F's R/R of 1:2.14 is a direct response to the PFE lesson.** After PFE's sub-1:1 R/R produced a loss, we established a minimum 1:1.5 ratio. F at 1:2.14 exceeds this by a wide margin. If this trade wins, the favorable ratio means the dollar gain is meaningful. If it loses, the tight $0.35 stop limits damage to ~$28.

3. **79 shares at $12.63 is the ultimate expression of the "dollar exposure" principle.** The experiment learned from DVN (22 shares at $46 = good) vs PANW (6 shares at $168 = bad) that share count matters. F pushes this to the extreme — 79 shares means every penny of movement = $0.79 of P/L. A $0.75 move to target = $59.25. On a $1,000 position, that's nearly 6% return.

4. **Running two positions (ET + F) provides genuine diversification for the first time.** ET benefits from elevated energy activity. F benefits from consumer recovery. They're inversely correlated on the oil/peace thesis — if oil drops on a deal, F benefits and ET holds steady (toll-road). If oil spikes on war resumption, ET benefits and F faces headwinds but is protected by the stop.

5. **The market decoupling from oil is the single most important macro shift of the experiment.** The S&P hit records while Brent crossed $100. This was unthinkable in March when every oil spike crashed stocks. The decoupling means consumer/cyclical names like F can rally even if oil stays elevated, as long as earnings remain strong. This expands the universe of tradeable setups beyond the war-dependent trades that dominated the first half of the experiment.
