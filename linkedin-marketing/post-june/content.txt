Quick context if you missed the earlier posts: I'm running a live test to see whether an AI can autonomously direct short-term equity trades - fully autonomous on entry, stop loss, sizing, and exit - while I execute orders and supply daily market data. $1,000 per position. Claude picks everything else.

Now at 15 closed trades: 5 wins, 10 losses. 33% win rate. The average win ($56) beats the average loss ($36), so the math can still work - just hasn't yet.

The current NCLH trade is the whole thing in miniature. The thesis: as oil falls and the Strait normalizes after the Iran deal, cruise operators' fuel costs drop and bookings recover. Entered at $19.44. Stop trailed to $19.75 after the position moved in our favor. Premarket yesterday: $19.83. Eight cents.

The Strait re-closed Saturday. Iran said Israel's continued strikes in Lebanon violated the ceasefire terms. Oil fell anyway - Brent at $78, the lowest since the day the war started. The market is pricing in eventual normalization regardless of the near-term noise.

If the stop triggers: +$15.81, sixth win of the experiment. If it reaches $22: the experiment turns profitable for the first time.

The AI doesn't move the stop. Every time I've looked at the decision log wanting to adjust it "just in case," the answer is no: the stop is set at a structural level, moving it degrades the risk/reward. That might be the most useful thing I've taken from this so far - not the win rate or the sector patterns, but what it actually feels like to hold your own rules when money is on the line.
